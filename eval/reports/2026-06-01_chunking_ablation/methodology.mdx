# Chunking ablation — fixed_window vs transcript_segment (PROVISIONAL)

**Run date:** 2026-06-01
**Run IDs:** `chunking-ablation-fixe-b793d883` (fixed_window), `chunking-ablation-tran-831b4d84` (transcript_segment)
**Code path:** `chunking_ablation`
**Outcome:** ⚠️ **PROVISIONAL.** Gold-span sizing resolved (clips are answer
*regions*); the retrieval selector switched to **RegionIoU@5**, which now
discriminates and puts **fixed_window** ahead on every channel. Not locked: the
boundary tier (edge/standalone) is not cold-kappa-validated, so a full ADR-004
chunking decision still waits on it. No `lock_bakeoff_winner`, no ADR amendment.

## What changed since the first run (same date)

The first pass reported TR@5 = 1.00/1.00, Contained@5 = 0.00/0.00, IoU@1 ≈ 0.2 —
**no winner**, because all three metrics were dominated by the gold-span size,
not the strategy. The dev_gold single_clip spans average **108.3s** (62–155s).

We resolved the fork (the user's call): **the spans are intended answer
regions.** The 10 questions are deliberately multi-claim (4–5 `expected_claims`
each, distributed across the span — e.g. "what *two* reasons", "what planning
tools *and* states *and* finish gate"). Tightening them to 20–40s moments would
mean rewriting the questions into single-fact questions, which redesigns what the
eval tests. We kept the spans and questions as-is and fixed the **metric**
instead.

New selector: **RegionIoU@5** — IoU of the *union of the top-5 retrieved chunks*
(on the gold video) against the gold region. It rewards covering the whole region
without dragging in unrelated time, and works at region scale where the others
fail:

- TR@5 is saturated (a 108s region overlaps something at any granularity).
- IoU@1 sees only the rank-1 chunk, so it rewards one big chunk over several
  small ones — a size artifact, not strategy quality.
- Contained@5 needs a single wrapping chunk; no strategy produces one for 108s.

## Reading the result

fixed_window's five 30s chunks **tile** a ~108s region tightly (high
intersection, lean union). transcript_segment's one-or-two large chunks (avg
51–87s, p95 up to 156s) **overhang** the region, inflating the union and lowering
the IoU. The signal is consistent across every channel, which is what makes the
0.35-vs-0.25 gap credible at n=10 rather than noise on one fused ranking.

This also explains why IoU@1 was misleading: at rank-1, a single big chunk
overlaps the region more than a single small chunk, so IoU@1 favored
transcript_segment. RegionIoU@5 measures the strategy's *actual* localization
behavior (how well its top-k jointly cover the region), which is the property a
clip-retrieval product cares about.

## Decision

**fixed_window is the provisional retrieval-localization leader. Not locked.**

A full ADR-004 chunking decision is retrieval localization **plus** boundary
quality (EdgeSensibility / Standalone). The boundary judge (`boundary_v1`) has
only a provisional, non-independent edge-kappa (0.81 inflated; standalone ≈ 0.07)
and is not cold-validated. We do not publish a chunking-quality claim off an
unvalidated judge, so no winner is locked and ADR 004 is not amended this run.
n = 10 dev_gold is also small.

**Next, in order:**
1. Cold boundary-kappa pass (fresh ~20 clips, edge + standalone, no LLM
   suggestions) → validate `boundary_v1` → unlock the boundary tier.
2. Then combine RegionIoU@5 (retrieval) + boundary quality for an ADR-004
   chunking decision and consider locking.
3. Add corpus-fit strategies (slide_boundary; a cheap embedding-similarity
   semantic chunker) now that the retrieval selector discriminates.
4. Generator bakeoff #2 re-run on the 213-chunk substrate + citation accuracy.
